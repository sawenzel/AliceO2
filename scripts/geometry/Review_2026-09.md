# Deep review, 2026-09 — the mathematics and algorithms, read from the source

This document is a review of what the `swenzel/bvhsurfacesolid` branch has achieved
mathematically and algorithmically, written 2026-09-20 on return to the project after six weeks.
Unlike `CodeReview_Fable.md` and `_v2.md`, which were defect registers driving the work, this is
a *reading* review of the finished state: the actual code was read end to end —
`BoundedSurface.h` (6017 lines), `O2BVHSurfaceSolid.{h,cxx}`, `O2SurfaceSolidIO.cxx`, the
recognition and extraction core of `O2_CADtoTGeo.py`, and the oracle scripts — and the central
formulas were re-derived independently rather than taken from the comments.

**Method and its limit.** This review was produced without a compiled build (the alidist stack
was rebuilding at the time), so every mathematical statement below is verified by derivation and
reading, while every *measured* number is quoted from the branch's own documents and marked so.
Appendix A is the re-verification of the recorded state and is to be filled in when the build
lands; until then the recorded numbers are the branch's claims, not this review's.

**The verdict, first.** The mathematical core is sound and unusually well built, because every
numerical decision in it is dimensionally honest — tolerances are lengths in cm applied through
the surface's first fundamental form, or dimensionless ratios in units of machine epsilon, and
nowhere any longer a bare constant compared against a quantity of unknown units. The algorithms
are correct by construction where that is possible (closure by identity counting, safety by
proven lower bounds, capacity by closed-form contour integrals) and validated by independent
oracle where it is not. The honest limits are also clearly drawn: rational-B-spline trims make
capacity numeric rather than exact, the B-spline trim boundary enters navigation as a flattened
polyline at 1e-5, and free-form *surfaces* are not represented at all. I consider the branch to
be at a defensible sweet spot for Geant4-facing use, and the remaining risks are named in §6.

---

## 1. What was built, in one page

Three representations of a CAD solid, tried in order of strength, with the choice recorded per
part: **CSG** (native ROOT primitives and composites, exact analytically), the
**`O2BVHSurfaceSolid`** (the part's real trimmed analytic B-rep faces carried into TGeo, exact
per face), and **`O2Tessellated`** (a triangle mesh, the fallback). Around them, four
instruments: an **OpenCascade oracle** answering the exact sample sets the harness generates; an
**acceptance gate** scoring every representation of every part against that oracle per column;
an **X-ray transport benchmark** that steps rather than asks single-shot questions; and an
**assembly oracle** whose per-ray answer is an occupancy sequence, not a boolean. The kernel
carries a non-BVH twin of every accelerated query (`Contains_Loop`, `DistFrom*_Loop`,
`Safety_Loop`) with a bit-identity contract, so the acceleration is separately falsifiable from
the geometry.

The recorded state (2026-08-09, to be re-verified in Appendix A): 113 unit cases green; ladder
fixtures 10/10; Bagger 13/13 with **zero** tessellated parts and gate exit 0; ALICE3 20 of 55
definitions exact, zero unexplained oracle disagreements on every column of both corpora, 13822/
13822 ALICE3 rays identical to OpenCascade.

## 2. The mathematical core, assessed

### 2.1 The parametric metric — the load-bearing idea

Every surface family exposes its first fundamental form (`parametricMetric`), and every
tolerance ever applied to a parametric separation goes through it. This is the single idea that
retired a whole class of historical defects: the (u, v) domains mix radians and centimetres with
a different mixture per family, so `du² + dv²` has no physical meaning, and the old constants
compared against it silently meant 1e-5 cm of arc on a 1 cm cylinder and 1e-3 cm on a 100 cm
one. The forms themselves are the textbook ones (plane with a cross term for non-orthogonal
frames; r² for the cylinder azimuth; r²sin²θ vanishing at sphere poles; (R + r cos v)² for the
torus ring) and are written once as free functions shared by the kernel and the sidecar reader,
so the join rule cannot diverge between the two layers. Degenerate scales (poles, apices) are
handled in the conservative direction: where a parametric separation spans no length, only the
representation term of the boundary band survives, which is the correct answer.

### 2.2 Trim curves and containment in the parameter domain

`Curve2D` carries lines, circular arcs and clamped rational B-splines as one value type. The
B-spline machinery is Piegl–Tiller (`DersBasisFuns` specialised to first derivative), with three
details that each closed a real defect and are worth naming because they are where a naive
implementation fails:

- **Clamping is checked, not assumed** — an unclamped knot vector (what OCC writes for a
  tube-tube intersection before `SetNotPeriodic`) does not interpolate its end poles, so
  endpoints are evaluated rather than read off the poles (K1, with the loader's twin N2 fixed).
- **Flatness testing never straddles an interior knot and never uses a single midpoint probe** —
  a curve symmetric about its parameter midpoint defeats a midpoint probe exactly (measured:
  2e-7 apparent flatness against a real 0.16 bulge on `BoomCylinderInner`), and a closed curve
  has a zero-length chord however much curve lies between its endpoints, so a degenerate chord
  switches the criterion to distance-to-point (K4).
- **One seam vertex per join, fixed by the wire and substituted into both neighbours**
  (`setCanonicalEndpoints`), so winding and point-to-curve distance measure against the *same*
  polyline. Before this, the two disagreed by the seam drift, which is a defect no band can fix.

Point-in-wire classification is winding by half-open scanline crossings, computed **exactly**
for lines and arcs (arcs are split at their v-extreme angles so each sub-arc is v-monotone and
the crossing abscissa is closed-form) and against the flattened polyline for B-splines. The
on-boundary band is set from what the representation actually is — `kBSplineFlatness` for a
polyline-backed curve, the metric-converted `kTolerance` floor otherwise — because a 1e-9 band
around a 1e-5 approximation "is not a band, it is noise" (K5). The `RayHit::onTrimBoundary` flag
then records when a hit's in/out verdict rests on that tie-break rather than on the data, which
§2.5 consumes. This is careful, honest engineering: the tie-break is one-sided and the code says
so, quotes the measured overhang (1.0–1.9e-5 cm on `cyl_cross_cyl`, never undercutting), and
routes the ambiguity to a vote instead of pretending it away.

### 2.3 Capacity — closed forms via the divergence theorem, and Green's theorem for trims

The volume is `(1/3)∮ X·n dA` summed per face. For parametric-rectangle patches every family has
a closed form (the plane's is `(origin·n)·area/3`; the torus rectangle form integrates the full
Jacobian `r(R + r cos v)` expression). For wire-trimmed quadrics the surface integral is reduced
by Green's theorem to a **contour integral of an explicit antiderivative** along the trim wire.
I re-derived the cylinder and torus antiderivatives independently and they are correct — e.g.
for the cylinder, `X·n·|X_φ×X_h| = s·r·(C·e(φ) + r)` is independent of h, so
`F(φ) = (s r/3)(C·U sin φ − C·V cos φ + r φ)` integrates it exactly; the torus case works the
same way with only the `(a cos u + b sin u)` term carrying the ring angle. The quadrature is
Gauss–Legendre at order 20 with two mandatory subdivisions — at the curve's interior knots
(a B-spline is one polynomial only within a span) and at a quarter-turn cap on how far u
*travels* (`uVariation`, which reports travel rather than endpoint difference, because a closed
hole loop has identical endpoints and once received a single interval for a whole loop — the
entire five-session capacity residual). Seams are bridged explicitly, since a 1e-5 contour gap
is a first-order integral error against a 1e-6 target. Exactness is *accounted*, not claimed:
`capacityIsExact()` is false exactly where a rational span forces numeric quadrature.

The superseded 128×128 midpoint rule is kept as the independent check — the right decision,
because it needs nothing of the integrand but its value. Recorded residual after the fix:
4.9e-04 → 2.8e-07 relative.

### 2.4 Ray intersection — quadratics, and a quartic done properly

The quadrics are standard stable quadratics with tangential grazes suppressed
(`sameIntersection` clustering) so crossing parity is preserved. The torus is the hard case and
`solveQuarticReal` is, in my assessment, the strongest single piece of numerical work on the
branch. It is Ferrari's method — I verified the resolvent cubic
`m³ + p m² + (p²/4 − r)m − q²/8` and the quadratic factor pair
`z² ∓ √(2m) z + (p/2 + m ± q/(2√(2m)))` against the standard derivation — with three properties
that production quartics usually lack:

1. **All guards are dimensionless.** The root variable is rescaled by the Cauchy bound rounded
   *up to a power of two*, so every coefficient lands in [−1, 1] and the branch tests ("did q
   cancel to zero", "is the resolvent resolved") compare against `32ε` — a statement about
   arithmetic, not centimetres. The power of two makes the substitution exact: on any input the
   old code answered correctly, the new code computes the same quantities with shifted exponents,
   so the normalisation *cannot* change an answer — only the guards can. That converts inertness
   from an argument into a structural property.
2. **An unresolved resolvent is routed to the biquadratic branch, not to "no roots".** When the
   computed resolvent is below the noise floor of its own cubic, q is at the same noise level and
   the biquadratic is the better-conditioned answer for that input; returning nothing (the old
   behaviour) turned an ill-conditioned crossing into a missing wall. Measured consequence of the
   old guards: 0 roots returned for a ray 375 cm from a 0.1 cm tube on *unscaled* ALICE3 — the
   trigger is lever arm over feature size, not model scale.
3. **Root clustering serves parity.** A double root is deliberately returned as a near-equal
   pair; the torus caller clusters (even cluster = tangential graze, dropped; odd = one crossing
   at the cluster mean), the same convention the quadrics use, so tangencies cannot flip parity.
   Newton polish steps are bounded by the Cauchy bound |z| ≤ 2 instead of by a derivative
   tolerance in cm³.

Standing residuals, recorded and not yet implicated by any measurement: `sameIntersection`'s
`max(1., …)` floor (same defect class, unfixed), `kBSplineFlatness`'s trim-sliver family, and
the depression step as the conditioning ceiling (relative root spread 2e-4 returns nothing).

### 2.5 Containment — parity as a topological invariant, and honesty about when it is not

`Contains` is: BVH point-candidates for the on-surface band, then single-ray crossing parity
along a fixed skew direction — justified *as a measurement*: on a closed oriented 2-manifold
parity is direction-independent, and the sweep that licenses the fast path (143 000
point×direction trials over both corpora, one disagreement, since diagnosed to the trim-sliver
tie-break) was re-run when the closure criterion changed, because a criterion that admits more
solids is a criterion that moves solids onto this path. When a shot's parity rests on an
`onTrimBoundary` tie-break, or when the solid is not Reliable, the answer comes from a majority
vote over five golden-angle-spiral directions — with the shots that stand on the geometry
outvoting the tie-break-dependent shots, and only a genuine tie falling back to counting all
five. The spiral replaced a hand-picked triple after two of its directions proved correlated and
agreed on the wrong answer; five directions were chosen because the measurement said 50/55 at
three, 53/55 at five, 55/55 at thirteen — the vote mitigates a defect, it does not repair one,
and the code says so.

### 2.6 Safety and normals — provable bounds, asymmetric rounding

`Safety` returns the exact minimum of `distanceSqToPatch` over all faces, reached through an
ordered BVH descent whose node bound (point-to-AABB distance) is a genuine lower bound on every
patch in the subtree — the argument is written out per family: every `distanceSqToPatch`
realises on the patch's *untrimmed window*, and each family's cover boxes enclose exactly that
window (a sphere and a torus therefore cover the whole surface of revolution, however small the
trim, because their kernels project radially and never look at the trim). The traversal is
required to reproduce the plain loop **bit for bit**, including the tie (lowest index wins,
which `ComputeNormal` turns into a normal), and the recorded verification is 28 000 point checks
with zero bound violations. Every inequality errs downward and the final answer is rounded down
one ulp, because a too-small safety costs one extra boundary computation while a too-large one
walks through a wall. The traversal is seeded from 24 on-patch anchor points (VecGeom's
tessellated trick), with an inflation argument for why the seed can never displace the true
winner; the opt-in approximate mode (`SetSafetySlack`) is a best-first traversal whose early
stop returns the smallest outstanding node bound — a guaranteed underestimate within (1−s) of
exact, so the approximation degrades step length, never correctness. Recorded numbers: 835 µs →
22.4 µs (BVH), 3.94 candidates/call after sub-patch covers, 127 ns for a far query at slack.

### 2.7 Closure — three generations, ending at identity

The closure story is the project's cleanest example of replacing a wrong *kind* of answer rather
than tuning a wrong value. Generation one counted quantized directed chords — which answers "did
two faces emit the same vertices", a question real CAD answers "no" for reasons that are not
gaps. Generation two measured rims as curves with a sagitta-aware match band (the band a chord
is judged in is the declared tolerance plus what two polylines of one shared curve can differ
by), with the deliberate two-band split: shared-or-open judged in the sagitta-widened band,
non-manifold judged in the declared tolerance alone, because widening the latter made every
chord near a corner non-manifold. Generation three — sidecar v3 — decides by **counting edge
identities**: every source edge must appear exactly twice with opposite sense; zero tolerance,
zero sampling; degenerate edges (apices, poles) excluded as points with no partner; the
all-or-nothing rule (identity applies only when *every* face states its edges) closing the
loophole where a silent face would look like a face with no missing neighbours. The geometric
measurement survives as what it always was — a measurement, `maxSharedEdgeDeviation` by
symmetric Hausdorff distance being the first defensible "how far apart are these two faces"
number. The recorded effect: parts failing on navigability 7/21 → 0/21, and the criterion is
immune to `kBSplineFlatness`, which under generation two made the verdict *worse* as the
geometry got better.

The known blind spot is stated in the code and the docs and is still open: **closure and edge
identity are sign-blind.** A face with an inward outward-normal reads closed and consistently
oriented (measured: three ALICE3 parts, nine inverted faces, all `reliable`). `CloseShape` warns
on negative signed volume — which catches the all-inverted case, not the mixed one — and the
per-face check ("no face's outward normal antiparallel to the source face's") is flagged in
three consecutive hand-overs and remains neither a harness flag nor a gate column. It should
become one before the next new corpus arrives.

### 2.8 The BVH — sub-patch covers, float boxes, and a two-pass prune

The acceleration layer is bvh2 over **cover boxes**: several exact per-chunk boxes per curved
surface (π/4 azimuthal chunks whose extents come from exact sinusoid range formulas, so a chunk
box hugs its arc to ~8% and carries no sampling slack) instead of one conservative box per face.
Leaves are one box each — the right call here, because a patch intersection costs vastly more
than a node test, unlike triangles. Boxes are widened by `kBVHBoxTolerance` (which dominates
every navigation tolerance, so a within-tolerance hit is never pruned) and rounded *outward*
through the double→float conversion. A per-query epoch marker dedups surfaces reachable through
several boxes, keeping hit multisets — and therefore parity — identical to the loop twins. The
distance query shrinks `ray.tmax` to the best candidate plus a cluster margin as it goes, and
handles the one soundness hole of that optimisation explicitly: if the nearest candidate turns
out to be a graze, the answer may lie beyond what pruning discarded, so the query re-runs once
without pruning. The two passes must return the same number, which keeps the optimisation an
optimisation.

## 3. The converter, assessed

The recognizer (`_recognize_analytic_surface`) is model selection over plane ⊂ sphere ⊂ cylinder
⊂ cone ⊂ free-form, with linear least-squares *proposing* each candidate (concurrent normal
lines for the sphere; the normal field's null singular vector for the cylinder axis; the apex
from `N·(P−A)=0` for the cone) and **one quantity deciding for all of them**: the achieved
geometric gap to the candidate surface, relative to the patch diagonal, against 1e-9. That
uniformity is the fix for a real latent bug this branch found in itself — cones scored by an
angle passed *vacuously* on near-rank-2 normal fields, accepting 184 ALICE3 faces whose cone
missed the real surface by up to 79 cm — and the acceptance is deliberately conservative: a
declined face costs coverage, a wrongly accepted one costs correctness. The self-test carries
negative controls (a recognizer without a negative control is worthless), and the shipped
recognizer was cross-checked against OCCT's `ShapeAnalysis_CanonicalRecognition` (agreement
within ten faces of ~1000, the converter stricter on both sides). The trim extraction carries
lines, arcs, ellipses (a conic *is* a rational quadratic B-spline — one widened set literal took
Bagger to 13/13) and B-splines into the sidecar; the edge table and per-curve `(edgeId, sense)`
implement v3; the sidecar format is backward compatible v1→v3 by construction (each version
appends; absence means "not stated" and degrades to the older verdict, never to a guess).
Placed primitives (Stream N) removed the self-union hack: a recognised primitive is emitted in
canonical frame with a `TGeoHMatrix` beside it, absence meaning identity, so every older
artefact still loads.

## 4. The methodology is an achievement of its own

Three design rules give this branch its credibility, and I would present them as results
alongside the geometry. **The oracle is the acceptance criterion, not a mesh** — every part is
scored against OpenCascade on the exact sample set the harness generated, per column, with
mismatch classes, and "zero unexplained disagreements" is the invariant defended through every
change. **Every accelerated path has a bit-identical twin** — BVH vs loop is falsifiable
separately from geometry vs oracle. **Aggregates say *that*, never *where*** — the instruments
grew per-face and per-ray localisers (`--rims`, `--edge-identity`, ordered crossing lists,
`DescribeContainsCrossings`) because five sessions of correct reasoning failed to find what one
localiser run then named. The documents record refuted premises instead of deleting them, which
is why this reading review could be done at all.

## 5. Findings from this reading

Nothing here changes the verdict; items 1–3 are new observations from the source, 4–5 are
standing items restated because they still gate the next phase.

1. **The recognition gap is scored on the same 9×9 sample grid that proposed the model.** A
   surface agreeing with a quadric at all 81 samples but deviating between them would be
   accepted with a recorded gap that never saw the deviation. For NURBS-encoded quadrics of
   modest degree this is unlikely to bite, and the oracle gate would catch the consequence
   downstream — but the criterion's own blind spot is cheap to close: score on a denser or
   half-cell-offset grid than the fit used. One-line risk, one-line fix, worth doing before the
   next corpus.
2. **`ComputeNormal`'s winner is chosen by a per-face lower bound.** For wire-trimmed quadrics
   `distanceSqToPatch` is the distance to the untrimmed window, so near a trimmed-away region
   face A's underestimate can beat face B's true distance and the normal comes from A. The BVH
   reproduces the loop bit for bit, so this is a semantic property of the definition, not of the
   acceleration; in practice `ComputeNormal` is called on boundary points where the point lies on
   the winning patch, and the gate has never flagged it. Record it as a documented approximation
   of the *definition*, so a future navigator use (mid-cell normals) does not discover it the
   hard way.
3. **The config knobs are plain globals.** `gRayTMaxPruning`, `gSafetySlack`,
   `gSafetyBoundUnsound` are non-atomic file-scope globals written through setters. Set-once
   before transport is fine; toggling during threaded transport would be a data race. One
   sentence in the header ("configure before transport") or `std::atomic` closes it.
   (`CurveWire::signedArea`'s missing seam bridges are already recorded at
   `integrateOverCurveTrimByParts` as off-path with a bounded residual; the wires it orients are
   far above `kAreaTolerance`, so I agree with the recorded disposition.)
4. **The face-normal gate column is still missing** (third consecutive hand-over, see §2.7).
   Closure and edge identity are sign-blind; the check exists as a diagnosis procedure and
   belongs in the harness and the gate before any new corpus is converted.
5. **The `auto`-mode fallback policy is still the one live production risk**: an exact part
   whose reliability is not `Reliable` ships with a warning instead of falling back to
   tessellated (`required` correctly throws). ~20 lines, Stream E step 3, still open.

## 6. The honest gaps, unchanged and confirmed

- **Free-form surfaces** are not represented (19 of 55 ALICE3 solids, 1373 faces). The genuine
  remainder and the largest effort; any implementation must report its achieved tolerance rather
  than claim the analytic path's exactness.
- **16 recognised-quadric solids fail on trims** whose boundary is transcendental in the (φ, h)
  chart; the exact route is arrangement-cell enumeration (research-grade, `Stream_R` §9.3), and
  the standing bargain — those parts ship tessellated — is now evidence-backed, both cheap
  alternatives having been measured and closed (1/15 and 1/16).
- **The tessellated fallback can be *invalid*, not merely inaccurate** (`MeshHealing.md`), and
  validity is non-monotone in precision. This matters more, not less, now that tessellation is
  the answer for 35 ALICE3 solids.
- **The source models are not legal worlds** (positive shared volume between placed solids:
  Bagger 3/78 pairs, ALICE3 ≥7/378 in the one censused sub-assembly), the ALICE3 census scale is
  unmeasured, and `TGeoManager::CheckOverlaps` does not work on this shape (starved by default,
  wrong when fed) — so there is no working overlap check on the representation we ship. The
  first is the model owner's decision; the second is ours to fix.
- **oTOF is unreachable** (XCAF traversal sees 3 of 62 628 solids), and it is the corpus placed
  primitives would pay best on.
- **Performance**: the surface solid trails an equivalent `TGeoCompositeShape` by 40–145×, with
  61% of `Contains` in `Curve2D::closestPoint` — the shared hot spot of containment, safety and
  normals, and the target the sub-patch BVH was built to shrink from the candidate side.
